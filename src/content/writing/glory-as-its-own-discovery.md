What consciousness discovers tends to glory as its own discovery.

That is the sentence I wrote down. What I mean by it is this: a person finds
something, understands it inside the limits of what he can see, and then
treats that understanding as if it were the whole of the matter. He validates
it against his own perception. He holds it up. He calls it a high and
wonderful achievement. And because he found it, he glories in it as if the
finding made it his.

The conscious mind is very good at this. It confuses the thing discovered
with the source of the thing. It promotes what it has grasped, and it does so
with real ignorance of where that grasp came from, and of how little of the
whole it actually holds. Often it is only holding up its own limitation, and
calling the limitation wisdom.

There is so much more that has not been discovered inside that limited
perception. Wisdom, love, knowledge, understanding, invention, creation:
depths that make our proudest findings look trivial by comparison. The
magnitude is unfathomable. Yet a person can live as if the small circle he
has mapped is the map, and as if standing inside that circle makes him wise.

That is the distraction. Not curiosity itself. Not learning. The glorying.
The way the discovery becomes a mirror, and the mirror becomes the point, and
the source is forgotten, ignored, or never looked for in the first place. We
become consumed with ourselves. We live day to day in pursuit of our own
devices, and that pursuit is not toward life. It is toward our own
destruction.

I am not writing this as a spectator. The pattern is easy to see in "them."
It is harder, and more useful, to see it in myself: the satisfaction of
having figured something out, the quiet promotion of that figuring, the way
attention narrows to what I can claim as mine. The corrective is not to stop
discovering. It is to stop mistaking the discovery for the source, and to
stop mistaking a limited grasp for the depth that remains.

Unless the Lord builds the house, the builders labor in vain. That is already
the name of this section, and it is the right word for what I am trying to
say. Whatever I find, I did not invent the ground I found it on. Whatever I
understand, I understand inside a mind that did not make itself. Glory that
stops at the discovery stops too soon.
