title: "Two agents, one folder, and a near miss"

I had two Claude sessions working on the same project tonight. One was turning research about local businesses into personalized report pages. The other was going through my homepage with me, line by line, fixing wording.

I knew both were running. What I hadn't thought about is that they were working in the same folder on my Mac. Neither one knew the other existed.

I found out when the copy session listed the changed files and there were more of them than it had touched.

Nothing was actually broken. The two sessions had worked on different files, except for one they both edited, and even there they'd been in different parts of it. That was luck more than design.

The near miss was what came next. I told the copy session I was about to save and upload my work. It stopped me, and it was right to.

The two things it caught:

Saving "everything that changed" would have bundled up the other session's finished work along with an evening of homepage edits nobody had reviewed. On this project, uploading is not a save. Uploading rebuilds the live site. The whole evening would have gone public in one move.

And switching to the main version of the project, which is the normal thing you do before merging work together, would have made a pile of files disappear from the folder. They only existed on the branch the other session had been working on. They'd have come back, but I'd have spent twenty minutes at midnight thinking I'd deleted a day of work.

The fix was small. The report session wrote down where it had got to before it closed, including which files belonged to the other session and were not to be touched. The copy session read that, saved only its own four files, and left everything else alone.

What I'd do differently: decide up front which session owns which files, and have each one say so out loud when it starts. Two agents in one folder is fine. Two agents in one folder that both think they're alone is how you lose an evening.

The other thing I'm keeping is the habit that saved it, which is asking before saving rather than after. The session had every instruction it needed to just go ahead. It stopped and told me what I was about to do instead, and that turned a bad night into a five minute conversation.
