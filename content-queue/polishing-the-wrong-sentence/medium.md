# I polished the wrong sentence three times

My homepage lists what I do. Most of it is about websites: checking your pages for things that aren't true anymore, checking whether Google and Yelp agree with your site, rebuilding the site when the layout is the real problem.

Then, with no warning, there are two things about AI. Rules for your team using AI at work, and help if an AI answer about your business goes wrong in public.

I noticed the jolt and asked for a divider, a small line to sit between the two groups so the AI part didn't arrive out of nowhere.

The first attempt said the things below were for specific situations. Reasonable, but it needed work. The second tried a before-and-after framing. Closer, still not right. The third dropped a word I didn't like and rearranged the rest.

Each one was a bit better and none of them fixed it, and I couldn't say why.

The reason turned out to be simple. Every version was labeling how often you'd buy the thing. Ongoing versus one-time. But the jolt had nothing to do with how often. The jolt was that the reader had been reading about websites for the whole page and suddenly AI showed up.

That's a subject problem, and no label about timing was ever going to fix it, no matter how well written.

I'd been asking for a better sentence when I should have been asking what the sentence was for.

Once that was clear, the fix took one pass. Give the AI items their own section with a heading that says what they're about, so you see the change of subject before you get to it.

There was a second version of the same mistake sitting right above it. The first group of services had a small label over it that just said Services. Fine on its own. But once the AI group had a label of its own, the two sat side by side, and a reader comparing them would reasonably conclude that the AI things weren't services.

That's not what I meant, and I'd never have caught it looking at either label by itself. Two labels next to each other say something about each other.

The part I want to remember is the tell. When a sentence keeps almost working, and every rewrite is slightly better and still wrong, the sentence usually isn't the problem. It's answering a question nobody asked.
