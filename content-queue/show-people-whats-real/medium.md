Tonight the business site is live.

Not as a stack of tools. As a plain claim an owner can recognize: your business changes. Does your website?

I spent a long stretch calling the work AI consulting and automation. That may still be true in the workshop. It was the wrong front door. What a stranger can check in five minutes is whether the public version of their business still matches the real one. Hours. People. Links. Promises that aged out while nobody was looking.

So the page leads with that gap. The door is a free mini report on a public website. After that, a monthly accuracy pass you can cancel before the next month. Two named plans, early rates that stick if you start now. A one-time consistency sweep when the same facts disagree across Google, Yelp, and the rest. A website build or rebuild when the layout itself is the problem. An AI policy pack and forensics on the shelf when those fit. The heavier client operating-system work stays off this page on purpose.

What shipped is the door, not the first client. The next honest motion is sending real mini reports to real sites, not polishing another rung because the homepage felt unfinished.

A little polish remains. The ship gate does not.
