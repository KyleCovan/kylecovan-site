# Why the builds page opened with Ecclesiastes

For a while, the page where I kept the things I was building opened with a verse.

> Cast your bread upon the waters, for you will find it after many days.
>
> Ecclesiastes 11:1, ESV

It sat above the heading, not as the heading. That was deliberate. The first thing you read was Scripture. The thing Google and a screen reader use to decide what the page is about stayed plain: the things I build, finished or not.

I did not want a search result, or a share card, or a heading list, to announce a verse about bread and water when the page was about software. The verse was for the person who arrived. It was not the name of the page.

## Why that verse

Building in public is an act of sending something out before you know what it will become.

You write up a half-finished system. You say what broke. You leave the unfinished things on the page on purpose. You do not know who will read it, or when, or whether anything comes back. Ecclesiastes already had words for that: cast the bread, and trust that after many days you will find it.

That is why the verse belonged over a build log and not over a polished portfolio. A portfolio is a finished claim. A log is bread on the water.

## The title I did not use

I almost named the page "Upon the Waters."

Three words. A clear nod to the verse. It would have sat in the nav next to "Unless the Lord."

That is exactly why it did not ship.

Two allusive Scripture names in one row, both three words, both prepositional, both starting with U, stop working as names. A visitor stops reading them as destinations and starts reading them as a theme. The names cancel each other out.

So the page kept a plain title, and the verse itself carried the phrase. "Upon the Waters" is still unspent. I am holding it for a series, a section, or a newsletter later, when it can be the only name of its kind in the room.

## What changed

The site now has one writing door. Essays and the dated logs live in the same house, under Unless the Lord. The old builds URLs still work; they lead into that house. They are not a second blog.

When the builds page stopped being a door, Ecclesiastes 11:1 went with it. Psalm 127:1a stayed on Unless the Lord. I did not move the Ecclesiastes line onto that page. Two verses fighting for the same masthead would have repeated the same mistake as two Scripture names in the nav.

The bread is still being cast. It just lives in one house now.
