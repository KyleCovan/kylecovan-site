I canceled my Anthropic Claude subscription today and moved everything to Cursor.

One AI surface for planning, drafting, and building. Not two.

That created a temptation worth naming: the obvious move would have been to copy my AI operating system into every Cursor project. Keep the brain close. That would have been wrong.

Two copies of a brain drift. You update one and forget the other. Within a few weeks you have two systems that are supposed to be one.

The better move was a thin pointer instead of a copy. My AI OS is plain markdown files. They're readable from anywhere with a path. So I wrote a User Rule for Cursor that points at those files when needed. Priorities, voice samples, decision log. Read from the source, never duplicated.

The files don't care which tool reads them. Only the delivery layer changed.

That's the pattern worth knowing if you're building something like this. When you switch tools or surfaces, the temptation is to replicate your system. The cleaner approach is to build surface-agnostic files and teach each surface to find them.

One brain. One pointer. No drift.
