I had two AI coding sessions working on the same project last night. One building report pages, one going through my homepage with me.

I knew both were running. I hadn't thought about the fact that they were working in the same folder, and that neither one knew the other existed.

I found out when one of them listed the changed files and there were more of them than it had touched.

Nothing was broken. They'd worked on different files, and the one file they both edited, they'd edited in different places. That was luck, not design.

The near miss came next. I said I was about to save and upload my work. The session stopped me.

Two things it caught:

Saving "everything that changed" would have bundled the other session's finished work in with an evening of edits nobody had reviewed. On this project, uploading isn't a save. It rebuilds the live site. The whole evening goes public in one move.

And switching to the main version of the project, which is the normal step before merging, would have made a pile of files vanish from the folder. They only existed on the other session's branch. They'd have come back, but I'd have spent twenty minutes at midnight thinking I'd deleted a day of work.

The fix was small. The first session wrote down where it got to before closing, including which files weren't its own. The second read that and saved only its four.

What I'd do differently: decide up front which session owns which files, and have each one say so when it starts.

Two agents in one folder is fine. Two agents in one folder that both think they're alone is how you lose an evening.

The habit that saved it was asking before saving instead of after. The session had everything it needed to just go ahead. It stopped and told me what I was about to do instead.
