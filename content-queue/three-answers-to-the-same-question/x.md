**Thread**

1/
My business checks websites for things that aren't true anymore.

Last night I found three different answers on my own site to one question: what does my free report include?

2/
The top of the page: it comes with the fix.

The how-it-works section: it's a list of problems.

The FAQ: sounds like the fix is a separate conversation you have to ask for.

3/
Then I opened the reports I actually send people.

Every finding comes with the wording to fix it.

So the product was better than two of my three descriptions of it.

4/
Nobody sits down and decides to contradict themselves.

You write about the same thing in three places over a few weeks. Each sentence is true enough on the day. They drift.

There's no single edit where it went wrong.

5/
An hour later, same pattern in my internal notes.

One section: never call these pages private, because not-indexed and not-private are different things.

400 words later: "pages are private links."

6/
The wrong version was in the section you'd skim to if you were in a hurry.

7/
The lesson isn't about websites.

Anywhere you say something twice, the copies eventually disagree. Not a discipline problem. Just what happens.

8/
The only fix is reading them side by side.

Which is the exact service I sell to other people.

I had never done it to my own site.
