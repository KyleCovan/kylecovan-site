My business checks websites for things that are no longer true. Old hours, staff who left, numbers that changed.

Last night I found three different answers on my own site to one question: what does the free report include?

The top of the page said it comes with the fix.
The how-it-works section said it was a list of problems.
The FAQ implied the fix was a separate conversation.

Then I opened the reports I actually send. They include the wording to fix every finding.

So the product was better than two of my three descriptions of it. One of them was talking customers into expecting less.

Nobody decides to contradict themselves. You write about the same thing in three places over a few weeks, each sentence is true enough on the day, and they drift apart. There's no single edit where it went wrong.

An hour later I found the same thing in my internal notes. One section said never describe those report pages as private, because not-indexed and not-private are different things. Four hundred words later, a bullet point called them private links.

The wrong version was in the part you'd skim to if you were in a hurry.

The lesson has nothing to do with websites. Anywhere you say something in more than one place, the copies eventually disagree. It isn't a discipline problem. It's just what happens.

The only fix is going back and reading them side by side, which is exactly the service I sell to other people and had never once done for myself.

Where does your own business describe itself more than once?
