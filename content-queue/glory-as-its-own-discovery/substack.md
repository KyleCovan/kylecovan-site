# What consciousness discovers tends to glory as its own discovery

I wrote that sentence down, and then I talked around it until the shape of the thought got clearer. This is the first draft of that shaping. I will edit it before it goes anywhere final.

What I mean is simple, and it is not flattering.

A person discovers something. He comes to understand it. He validates that understanding inside the limits of his own perception. Then he treats what he has perceived, inside those limits, as a high and wonderful achievement. He glories in it. He becomes distracted by it. And somewhere in that glorying, he confuses the discovery with the source. He promotes what he found while remaining ignorant of where it came from, and of how much still sits outside the circle he can see.

Often what he is holding up is not wisdom. It is the edge of his own limitation, polished until it looks like a summit.

That is the part that stays with me. Not that discovery is wrong. Learning is not the enemy. The glorying is. The way a find becomes a mirror. The way the mirror becomes the point. The way attention narrows to what can be claimed as mine.

There is, by far, more wisdom beyond what we consider ourselves wise for having grasped. Love, knowledge, understanding, invention, creation. Magnitude we do not reach. Depth we do not sound. Next to that, our proudest findings are trivial. And still a person can live day to day consumed with himself, in pursuit of his own devices, as if the small mapped circle were the world.

I do not write that as a lecture aimed at someone else. The pattern is easy to spot in "them." It is harder, and more useful, in myself. The satisfaction of having figured something out. The quiet promotion of that figuring. The forgetting that the ground I found it on was not invented by me, and that the mind doing the finding did not make itself.

The corrective is not to stop discovering. It is to stop mistaking the discovery for the source, and a limited grasp for the depth that remains.

Unless the Lord builds the house, the builders labor in vain. Whatever I find, glory that stops at the discovery stops too soon.

Kyle
