I spent a stretch of the day ranking what I can actually sell. Not inventing a catalog. Looking at the work I already do well, and asking which of those pieces a small business would pay for on purpose.

Two answers came up clear.

**Quiet rot, every month**

Websites go stale the way a porch light burns out. Nobody notices the day it dies. Customers notice weeks later, when the hours are wrong, the staff page is a ghost town, or a button goes nowhere.

I can find that drift. I can fix the top of the list. I can send a short report of what moved. That is a monthly accuracy pass. I'm calling the product line Entropy Retainers. The door in front of it is a free mini rot report on a real public site. Cheap to start. Honest about what it is.

**When AI makes a mess**

The other offer is different. It is not weekly. It waits for a bad day.

Someone's chatbot promised the wrong thing. A summary leaked something it shouldn't. A tool left a trail nobody can read. The owner or their lawyer needs a plain timeline and a memo, not another product pitch.

That is AI Incident Forensics. Rebuild what happened from the evidence they already have. Flat fee. Not legal advice. Triggered demand. It sits on the shelf until the phone rings for that reason.

**One ladder, not a pile**

For a while those looked like competing products. They aren't. They are rungs on one stack: free rot report, then the monthly accuracy retainer, then a website rebuild when trust is real, with a policy pack and forensics available when they fit. The heavier client AI operating system work stays off the public site for now. It is real work. It is not the front door.

Outreach for this ladder is national and remote. The work lives on public websites. Geography was a weaker constraint than I had been treating it.

**What the ranking was for**

The useful part was not the names. It was forcing the question: which of my strengths map to a job a stranger will pay for without a long trust runway?

Finding quiet mistakes on a live site is one of those. Reconstructing a messy AI incident in plain English is another. Both came out of looking at what I already do, not out of inventing something fashionable.

The site has to say this ladder before it ships. After that, the work is hunting real sites with the ladder in view, not building a third offer because ranking felt productive.
