## 1. Onesimus, and why the operating system is named after him

**The strongest story Kyle told on August 3, and it nearly ended up as a
footnote in a maintenance log.**

Onesimus is a man in the New Testament, from the letter to Philemon. He was a
runaway servant who found the Apostle Paul, spent time with him, and was sent
back to Philemon carrying the letter that now bears Philemon's name, asking that
he be received not as a servant but as a brother. His name meant useless, and
the letter turns on him becoming useful.

Kyle named his AI operating system Onesimus for that: useless to useful, lost to
found. The book is under a page long.

Why it deserves its own post rather than a paragraph: it is the only place where
the faith running through the rest of the site and the engineering meet in one
object. The nav already reads "Unless the L**ORD**". This is the post where the
two halves of the site explain each other.

Where it lands: `/writing/`, untagged, so it gets its own URL. It is not a build
log entry. It is about why the thing is called what it's called.

## 2. The three risks of putting agent files in your notes vault

Blast radius, version control mismatch, and noise, with the boundary that fixes
all three. Covered in compressed form in the "Four AI operating systems" entry,
but that entry is a story about a discovery, not a guide.

This is the most searchable thing Kyle has said all week. People are actively
asking whether agent instructions belong in Obsidian, and there is a real answer
with a real test: the operating system should read the vault, not depend on it.
If the folder only works because of links into forty private notes, the boundary
is wrong.

Worth writing as a genuine how-to. Would earn its own URL under `/writing/`.
